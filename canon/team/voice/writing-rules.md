Write like a person who knows the subject and respects the reader's time.

**No em dashes.** Use a period or a comma. This one is absolute because it is the
single clearest tell of generated prose.

**No rule of three.** "Faster, safer, and easier" is padding pretending to be
rhythm. Say the one thing that is true.

**No filler openers.** Not "In today's fast-paced world", not "It's worth noting
that", not "Let's dive in". Start with the claim.

**Sentence case headings.** Not Title Case Like This.

**Concrete over abstract.** "A supplier in Lagos waits ninety days" beats
"payment delays affect SMEs". A number, a place, a name.

**Name the failure.** Our best writing describes what went wrong and what it
cost. "The escrow released five minutes after the container left the port" is
worth more than "we improved release timing".

**Never claim a feature that is not shipped.** Every capability sentence resolves
to a canon fact with a live status. If it is on the roadmap, say so in the future
tense or leave it out.

**Short sentences carry weight.** A long one is fine when the idea needs it. Two
long ones in a row is a paragraph nobody finishes.

### Before and after

Before: "Karwan leverages cutting-edge blockchain technology to deliver a
seamless, secure, and scalable trade finance experience."

After: "Money sits in escrow until the work lands. The supplier can see it exists
before shipping."

Before: "We've enhanced our reputation system with new anti-gaming measures."

After: "A wallet used to reach the financing tier without completing a single
deal. Now a tier has to be backed by settled work."
