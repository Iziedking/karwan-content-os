Six rules, in order of how often they get broken.

**Calm and legible.** Karwan is where strangers put money in escrow with each
other, so the interface earns trust: calm like a good financial product, never
a generated template. One job and one primary action per screen.

**Money is the loudest thing.** The amount is the largest line, in tabular
figures with the currency written out: `1,200.00 USDC`, never `$1,200`. Next
loudest is the next action and whose move it is.

**One accent, used once.** Lime is for the one primary action and positive
money state. A page with three lime elements has no primary action.

**Sentence case, plain words.** No shouting labels. Brackets and mono are only
for technical metadata such as references and proof rows. Request and offer,
never brief or bid. No em dashes.

**Hairlines, not stacked cards.** Facts sit in label and value rows separated by
thin lines. Forms stay in a narrow reading column.

**Honest states and no internals.** Sending is never shown as failed. Chain ids,
addresses and protocol names stay behind "Proof on Arc". Motion only explains
change and respects reduced motion.
