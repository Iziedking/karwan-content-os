**Escrow.** A contract that holds the buyer's money while the work happens. It
releases against delivery, in milestones, and nobody can pull funds out of turn.

**Milestone.** One slice of a deal. A deal splits into two to five. The last one
always needs the buyer to click.

**Credit passport.** A public page built from a business's settled deals and
repayment behaviour. It belongs to the wallet, so it moves with the business
rather than living on the platform.

**Factoring.** Selling an invoice for less than face to be paid now instead of on
settlement. The discount is the cost of the wait.

**Purchase-order financing.** Working capital advanced before delivery, against a
purchase order whose escrow is already funded.

**Receivable assignment.** Recording, on the escrow, that a financier is paid
ahead of the supplier at settlement. It is what makes financing non-recourse: the
financier is repaid by the contract rather than by chasing the supplier.

**x402.** A payment standard for machine-to-machine calls, used here so an agent
can pay a fraction of a cent for a single piece of data.

**USYC.** A tokenised T-bill fund. Idle balance is parked in it so capital earns
while it waits.
