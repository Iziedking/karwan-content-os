Signing up is one page in three parts: your account (an email and a code,
or a wallet), your Karwan tag, and a few questions about you. Nothing else is
asked to open an account: no name, address or ID. On mainnet, a licensed
partner runs an identity check once, and only when a feature needs it.

Karwan sets up your agents on its own when you create your account, so
there is nothing to activate. Then your agent asks what you want Karwan to
do: get paid for your work, sell products, buy or hire, run business deals,
or build a record clients trust. It asks only what those answers need: what
you do and your usual price on one screen, which it reads back to you as a
price range and delivery time, then whether you have clients and how to
reach you. A line from your agent on each screen says what the answer is for.

What you tell your agent sets up your agent and your public profile by
itself. It shows the result as "Here's your Karwan", with the first offer or
request it wrote from your words, and saves nothing until you say it looks
right. "Post it on the market now" is ticked; untick it to post later. The
same questions are asked in Telegram, in the same order, with one more at the
end: which country you are in. They can also be changed from a conversation
with your agent.

If you already agreed a deal with someone and they are paying you, send them
your deal link. It opens the deal with you as the seller; they add the amount
and terms you agreed and pay into the deal, and you accept before anything is
final. Your agent can give you the link.

Three answers change what your agents do. If you only work with clients you
already have, your selling agent stays off the market. If you choose "Ask me
first" on the summary, it does not make offers on its own: it tells you about
a request that fits and you decide. "How should we reach you" picks Telegram,
email or both. Money never moves without your confirmation.

This is built for testnet and not live yet.
